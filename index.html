The Detection Bias

Transit photometry finds planets by watching a star dim as a planet crosses in front of it. Giant planets block a lot of light and are easy to spot, but Earth-sized planets barely dim the star at all, so transit surveys often miss them.

DETECTION WAITING
Hot Jupiter · 1.02% drop · DETECTED Earth-like · 0.0084% drop · MISSED

Transit depth  =  (Rp / Rs)2 × 100

Hot Jupiter (11 / 109)2 × 100 = 1.02% DETECTED
Earth-sized (1 / 109)2 × 100 = 0.0084% = 84 ppm MISSED

The demo exaggerates the dip so you can see it. A real Earth-sized dip (about 84 ppm) sits below the roughly 1,000 ppm limit of ground telescopes, and right at the edge of what even Kepler and JWST can measure.

What this proves

  • Transit photometry is size biased: it favors big gas giants.
  • Earth-like planets are nearly invisible to current instruments.
  • That is why the search for Earth-like planets needs four more detection methods.

Four Detection Methods That Fix the Bias

How They Feed ExoRank

These four methods get around the size bias that hides Earth-like planets from transit surveys. Each one measures an input the ExoRank algorithm uses, so as next-generation telescopes collect more of this data, every ExoRank score gets more reliable.

Asteroseismology → Stellar Type Microlensing → Distance Spectroscopy → Biosignature Direct Imaging → Radius & Tidal Lock
Variable 4 · Stellar Type

Asteroseismology

The study of vibrations inside stars. Scientists measure sound waves trapped in a star to work out its age, stability and radiation output. That shows whether a star is calm enough to support a civilization, without needing any signal from the planet at all.

Variable 3 · Distance

Gravitational Microlensing

When a massive star passes in front of a distant star, its gravity bends and magnifies the light behind it. If a planet orbits the nearer star, it adds an extra spike of brightness, which can reveal Earth-sized worlds thousands of light-years away that other methods can't reach.

Variable 8 · Biosignature

Atmospheric Spectroscopy

As starlight passes through a planet's atmosphere, gases like oxygen and methane absorb specific colors of light. By measuring which colors are missing, astronomers can tell what the air is made of. It is the only current method that can show whether a planet has breathable air.

Variables 2 & 5 · Radius & Tidal Lock

Direct Imaging

A coronagraph blocks the glare of the host star so the planet itself can be photographed. That helps pin down the planet's size and shows whether one side always faces its star (tidal locking).

REAL-WORLD IMPACT

A Sustainable Search

ExoRank is built to add scientific value at essentially zero environmental cost.

Zero Physical Waste

It is fully digital. It needs no new hardware or satellite launches, so there is nothing to throw away.

Reuses Existing Data

It runs only on data NASA already collects and publishes in its archive, so no new observations are needed.

Scales With Science

When James Webb and later telescopes fill in missing values, the scores update on the next data fetch, and the AI briefing is one click away.

Plans Ahead for a Crisis

The goal is to find a habitable second home before climate change or another disaster makes it too late.
